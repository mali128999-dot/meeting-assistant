AI Meeting Minutes & Summaries

Taking minutes while you are also trying to follow the discussion is hard. Meeting Assistant writes AI meeting notes for you. It transcribes the call on your Windows PC and, when the meeting ends, produces an AI meeting summary you can read in a minute and share with your team.

What you get after each meeting

  • Full transcript: everything that was said, in order, with "them" and "me" kept separate so you can see who spoke.
  • Summary: a short overview of what the meeting was about.
  • Key points: the main topics and facts that came up.
  • Decisions: what was agreed, so it does not get disputed later.
  • Action items: who needs to do what, written as a simple list.

Both the transcript and the summary are saved on your computer, so you can open them later, copy them into an email or paste them into your own minutes template.

A meeting summarizer that does not need a bot

Many AI note takers send a bot into your call. Meeting Assistant does not. It captures the audio that plays on your PC and your microphone, so it works with Microsoft Teams, Zoom, Google Meet and any other meeting app, and it works with headphones. See the details for Teams meeting transcription.

Private by design

Speech is turned into text locally with Whisper, so your audio never leaves your PC. To write the summary, the transcript text is sent to an AI service. We do not store your meeting content. Read the privacy policy for the details.

How to use it for minutes

  1. Start the app and join the meetingThe window stays on top and shows the live transcript.
  2. Add context onceUse the "About me" setting to tell the AI your role, so the summary uses your field's wording.
  3. End the meetingThe transcript and AI summary are saved. Review them, fix anything that looks wrong, and send them on.

Accuracy: check before you send

Automatic transcription can mishear names, numbers and technical terms, especially with poor audio or strong background noise. An AI summary can also miss nuance. Treat the output as a strong first draft and check important decisions and figures before you circulate them as official minutes.
